
# Section 5: Applications of Artificial Intelligence

## 1. Learning Objectives

After completing this section, learners should be able to:

1. Explain how artificial intelligence (AI) is applied in education, healthcare, business, science and engineering, and art, music, and creativity.
2. Define important terms related to practical AI applications.
3. Describe the problem an AI application is designed to address.
4. Compare the benefits and limitations of AI applications.
5. Explain why human knowledge and judgment remain important when using AI.

## 2. Introduction: Why Do We Use AI?

Artificial intelligence (AI) refers to computer systems designed to perform tasks that normally require abilities associated with human intelligence. These tasks may include recognizing patterns, understanding language, making predictions, solving problems, and generating new content.

AI is useful when people need to process large amounts of information, identify patterns, automate repetitive tasks, or receive assistance with decisions. However, AI is not equally suitable for every problem. Its usefulness depends on the task, the quality of available information, the design of the system, and how people use its results.

An **AI application** is a practical use of an AI system to perform a task or help solve a problem.

For example:
- A learning platform recommends practice questions to a student.
- A healthcare system highlights a pattern in a medical image.
- A business uses AI to estimate future product demand.
- A scientist uses AI to analyze research data.
- An artist uses generative AI to explore visual ideas.

These applications have different purposes, and each has its own benefits, limitations, and responsibilities.

## 3. Key Terminology

| Term | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Algorithm | A set of instructions or procedures used to solve a problem. | Instructions used to sort information. |
| Data | Information used by a computer system. | Images, text, measurements, or sales records. |
| Machine learning (ML) | A method in which a computer system learns patterns from data to perform a task. | Learning patterns associated with different types of images. |
| Prediction | An estimate of an outcome based on available information. | Estimating how many products a shop may sell next week. |
| Automation | Using technology to perform a task with reduced manual effort. | Automatically sorting incoming customer requests. |
| Generative AI | AI designed to produce new content, such as text, images, music, or code. | Generating a draft of a poster. |
| Human oversight | Human checking, supervision, or intervention when an AI system is used. | A doctor reviewing an AI-supported result. |

Not every application uses the same type of AI. Some systems classify information, some make predictions, and generative AI creates new content.

## 4. AI in Education

### 4.1 How AI Is Used

AI can support teaching and learning by helping learners practise skills, receive feedback, and access educational materials.

Common applications include:

- **Personalized practice:** A learning platform recommends exercises based on a student's performance.
- **Writing assistance:** AI tools suggest ways to improve grammar, organization, or clarity.
- **Language learning:** AI can provide conversation practice, pronunciation feedback, or vocabulary exercises.
- **Accessibility:** Speech-to-text and text-to-speech systems can help some learners access information in different formats.
- **Question generation:** AI can generate practice questions for revision.

### 4.2 Worked Example: Supporting a Mathematics Student

Imagine that a student is learning linear equations.

The student answers these questions:

- Question 1: Solve \(x + 3 = 8\). Answer: \(x = 5\).
- Question 2: Solve \(2x = 12\). Answer: \(x = 6\).
- Question 3: Solve \(x - 4 = 7\). Answer: \(x = 3\).

The first two answers are correct, but the third should be \(x = 11\).

A learning system may detect that the student is making mistakes with subtraction equations and recommend additional practice.

Step by step:

1. The student submits answers.
2. The system compares the answers with the expected results.
3. It identifies a possible pattern in the mistakes.
4. It recommends exercises focusing on that skill.
5. The student practises and checks their understanding with a teacher or reliable explanation.

The system helps identify where the student may need more practice. However, one incorrect answer does not prove that the student has misunderstood the entire topic. The student may have made a simple calculation mistake.

### 4.3 Benefits and Limitations

**Benefits**
- Provides additional practice.
- Can offer quick feedback.
- Supports different learning needs.
- Helps teachers identify areas where students may need support.

**Limitations**
- AI explanations may be incorrect.
- Students may become too dependent on automated answers.
- Personal learning data needs protection.
- AI feedback may not fully understand a student's circumstances.

AI should support learning rather than replace independent thinking or teachers.

## 5. AI in Healthcare

### 5.1 How AI Is Used

Healthcare professionals may use AI systems to help analyze information and support their work.

Examples include:

- **Medical image analysis:** Highlighting patterns in X-rays or other medical images for professional review.
- **Health research:** Helping researchers analyze large datasets.
- **Clinical decision support:** Organizing information that may help professionals evaluate a situation.
- **Administration:** Assisting with appointment scheduling or organizing records.

A **medical image** is an image showing structures inside the body, such as an X-ray. A **clinical decision** is a decision related to a person's healthcare.

### 5.2 Worked Example: Reviewing a Medical Image

Imagine that an AI-supported system examines an X-ray and highlights an area that may need further investigation.

The process is:

1. A medical image is provided to the system.
2. The system analyzes patterns in the image.
3. It highlights an area for the healthcare professional to examine.
4. The professional reviews the original image and other relevant information.
5. The professional decides whether further examination or testing is appropriate.

The highlighted area is not a confirmed diagnosis. AI can make mistakes, and a professional must interpret the result in its proper context.

### 5.3 Benefits and Limitations

**Benefits**
- May help professionals review large amounts of information.
- Can highlight patterns that deserve closer examination.
- May support research and administrative work.

**Limitations**
- Incorrect results may affect important decisions.
- Unrepresentative data may lead to unequal performance.
- Medical information is sensitive and requires protection.
- AI cannot take full responsibility for a patient's care.

Healthcare AI must be evaluated carefully, with appropriate professional oversight and privacy safeguards.

## 6. AI in Business

### 6.1 How AI Is Used

Businesses use AI to help understand information, support customers, and plan operations.

Common applications include:

- **Demand forecasting:** Estimating how much of a product customers may buy.
- **Customer service:** Answering common questions through chatbots.
- **Fraud detection:** Identifying transactions that appear unusual and may require investigation.
- **Recommendation systems:** Suggesting products based on browsing or purchase patterns.
- **Document processing:** Helping organize or summarize business documents.

A **forecast** is an estimate of what may happen in the future. A **recommendation system** suggests items or information based on patterns or user preferences.

### 6.2 Worked Example: Estimating Product Demand

A shop wants to estimate how many umbrellas it may sell next week.

It reviews previous sales and notices that umbrella sales are often higher during rainy periods.

Step by step:

1. The shop collects historical sales information.
2. It considers relevant information, such as season and weather forecasts.
3. An AI system identifies patterns in the data.
4. It estimates possible demand for the coming week.
5. The shop manager reviews the estimate and decides how much stock to order.

The estimate can help the shop prepare. However, the prediction may be inaccurate if weather conditions change or an unexpected event affects demand.

### 6.3 Benefits and Limitations

**Benefits**
- Reduces time spent on repetitive tasks.
- Helps businesses organize large amounts of data.
- Can support planning and customer service.
- May help identify unusual patterns.

**Limitations**
- Predictions may be wrong.
- Customer data may be collected or used inappropriately.
- Automated recommendations can disadvantage some customers or sellers.
- Human employees may need to review unusual or complicated cases.

Businesses should evaluate whether an AI tool genuinely solves the problem and whether its risks are acceptable.

## 7. AI in Science and Engineering

### 7.1 How AI Is Used

Scientists and engineers use AI to assist with analysis, design, simulation, and problem-solving.

Examples include:

- **Scientific data analysis:** Identifying patterns in experimental measurements.
- **Engineering design:** Exploring possible designs or optimizing selected parameters.
- **Predictive maintenance:** Estimating when equipment may require inspection.
- **Environmental research:** Analyzing satellite images or measurements to monitor environmental changes.
- **Simulation support:** Helping researchers explore possible outcomes before conducting physical experiments.

A **simulation** is a model of a real or imagined process that allows people to study how it may behave under different conditions. **Predictive maintenance** uses information about equipment to estimate when maintenance may be needed.

### 7.2 Worked Example: Predictive Maintenance

A factory operates a motor that produces vibration and temperature measurements while running.

Step by step:

1. Sensors collect information about the motor.
2. The system compares the measurements with patterns associated with normal operation and possible faults.
3. It identifies an unusual change in vibration.
4. The system alerts maintenance staff to inspect the motor.
5. A qualified technician checks the equipment and decides whether maintenance is necessary.

This approach may help the factory identify problems earlier. However, unusual measurements do not always mean that a motor is about to fail. The technician must investigate before making a decision.

### 7.3 Benefits and Limitations

**Benefits**
- Helps analyze complex datasets.
- Can support early detection of equipment problems.
- Allows researchers to explore possible designs.
- May reduce time spent on repetitive analysis.

**Limitations**
- Poor-quality measurements can produce unreliable results.
- A simulation may not represent every real-world condition.
- Engineering systems still require testing and validation.
- Safety-critical decisions require suitable standards and professional judgment.

AI can assist scientific discovery, but its outputs must be tested against evidence.

## 8. AI in Art, Music, and Creativity

### 8.1 How AI Is Used

Generative AI can produce new content based on patterns learned from training data and instructions supplied by a user.

Applications include:

- **Visual art:** Generating draft images or exploring visual styles.
- **Music:** Producing musical ideas or assisting with composition.
- **Writing:** Brainstorming story ideas or creating an initial draft.
- **Video and animation:** Helping develop storyboards or visual concepts.
- **Design:** Exploring possible layouts, logos, or product concepts.

A **prompt** is an instruction or input given to an AI system. A **draft** is an initial version of work that can be reviewed and improved.

### 8.2 Worked Example: Designing a Poster

A student needs a poster for a school environmental campaign.

Step by step:

1. The student identifies the poster's purpose and audience.
2. The student writes a prompt describing the topic, style, and intended message.
3. A generative AI tool produces a draft design.
4. The student checks the text, images, factual claims, and visual clarity.
5. The student edits the design and ensures that the final work follows school rules and relevant copyright requirements.

The AI can help the student explore ideas, but the student remains responsible for the final result.

### 8.3 Benefits and Limitations

**Benefits**
- Helps users explore ideas quickly.
- Supports creative experimentation.
- Can make some design tasks more accessible.
- Produces starting points that people can refine.

**Limitations**
- Generated content may contain errors or unwanted details.
- The output may resemble existing work.
- Users must consider copyright, attribution, and the rules governing their work.
- Generated content may not express the creator's intended meaning accurately.

AI can be a creative assistant, but human choices, editing, and judgment remain important.

## 9. Comparing AI Applications

| Field | Problem addressed | Example AI application | Potential benefit | Important limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Education | Students need more practice or feedback. | Recommending exercises. | More targeted practice. | Feedback may be wrong. |
| Healthcare | Professionals need to review complex information. | Highlighting patterns in medical images. | Supports closer examination. | Does not confirm a diagnosis. |
| Business | Businesses need to plan stock or process requests. | Demand forecasting. | Supports planning. | Predictions may be inaccurate. |
| Science and engineering | Researchers need to analyze measurements or monitor equipment. | Predictive maintenance. | May identify unusual patterns early. | Measurements require interpretation. |
| Art, music, and creativity | Creators need ideas or initial drafts. | Generating a poster concept. | Encourages experimentation. | Requires review and responsible use. |

The best application depends on the problem being solved. A tool that is useful for brainstorming may not be appropriate for making a high-impact decision.

## 10. Worked Example: Is AI the Right Tool?

A university is considering an AI chatbot to answer frequently asked questions about course registration.

The team identifies the following needs:

- Students need quick answers about deadlines and procedures.
- Answers must be accurate and up to date.
- Personal student information must remain protected.
- Students must be able to contact a staff member when their situation is unusual.

Evaluation:

1. **Purpose:** The chatbot could answer common questions and reduce repetitive work.
2. **Information quality:** Its answers should use approved, current university information.
3. **Privacy:** It should not reveal personal student records to unauthorized users.
4. **Human support:** Complicated cases should be transferred to an appropriate staff member.
5. **Testing:** The university should test the chatbot before relying on it and continue checking its performance.

**Conclusion:** The chatbot may be appropriate for routine questions if its accuracy, privacy, and escalation process are properly managed. It should not be the only source of support for every student situation.

## 12. Learner Activity B: Evaluate an AI Application

A small business uses AI to forecast weekly sales. The system predicts that the business will sell 500 units of a product next week.

However, the business is about to launch a major promotion that was not included in the information used by the system.

Answer the questions:

1. Should the business accept the prediction without checking it?
2. What additional information could improve the estimate?
3. What should the manager do before deciding how much stock to order?

## 14. Key Takeaways

- AI is used in education, healthcare, business, science and engineering, and creative work.
- Applications should be evaluated according to the problems they are intended to solve.
- AI may support analysis, predictions, automation, and content creation.
- AI outputs can be inaccurate, biased, incomplete, or unsuitable for a particular situation.
- Privacy, safety, fairness, and accountability should be considered.
- Human expertise remains important when interpreting AI results and making consequential decisions.
