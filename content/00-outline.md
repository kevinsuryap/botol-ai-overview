---

## 1. Module Overview

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is increasingly used in education, healthcare, business, science, and everyday life. However, beginners may not understand how AI works, what different AI technologies do, or why AI-generated information should be evaluated carefully.

The **AI Overview** module introduces learners to the fundamental concepts of AI, its major approaches, practical applications, limitations, and responsible use.

The module follows a progressive learning sequence. Learners first develop an understanding of AI and its basic concepts, then explore different AI approaches and generative AI. They subsequently examine real-world applications, identify potential risks, learn responsible practices, and apply their knowledge through practical activities.

The aim is to help learners understand not only what AI can do, but also how to evaluate and use it appropriately.

---

## 2. Intended Learning Outcomes

By the end of this module, beginners should be able to:

1. **Explain** the meaning of Artificial Intelligence and identify examples of AI in everyday life.
2. **Describe** basic AI concepts, including data, algorithms, models, training, and predictions.
3. **Distinguish** between symbolic, connectionist, and behavior-based approaches to AI at an introductory level.
4. **Explain** the basic purpose of generative AI, large language models (LLMs), and prompts.
5. **Identify** how AI is applied in different industries and everyday situations.
6. **Recognise** common AI limitations and risks, including inaccurate information, hallucinations, bias, privacy concerns, and reliability issues.
7. **Demonstrate** responsible AI use by checking information, protecting personal data, and following academic integrity rules.
8. **Apply** their knowledge to simple scenarios by identifying appropriate AI uses, evaluating AI-generated answers, and explaining their decisions.

These outcomes provide a foundation for selecting content and designing learning activities throughout the module.

---

## 3. Prerequisite Knowledge

This module is designed for beginners. No previous knowledge of AI, machine learning, or programming is required.

Learners should ideally have:

- Basic computer and internet skills.
- The ability to read and understand simple English explanations.
- Basic familiarity with searching for information online.
- Willingness to compare information, ask questions, and think critically.

Basic programming knowledge is optional. Technical concepts should be introduced through simple explanations and practical examples rather than complex mathematics or code.

## 5. Detailed Section Outlines

### Section 1: Introduction to AI

**File:** `01-introduction-to-ai.md`

**Purpose:** Introduce learners to AI and establish a foundation for the rest of the module.

**Topics and subtopics:**
- What is Artificial Intelligence?
- A brief history and development of AI.
- AI in everyday life.
- Common examples of AI systems.
- Why understanding AI matters.

**Planned activities:**
- Identify AI tools encountered in daily life.
- Discuss whether familiar digital services use AI.
- Answer introductory questions about AI.

**Expected learning outcome:** Learners can explain what AI is and recognise common examples.

**Status:** Outline only.

### Section 2: Basic AI Concepts

**File:** `03-basic-ai-concepts.md`

**Purpose:** Explain the basic concepts needed to understand how AI systems operate.

**Topics and subtopics:**
- Data and information.
- Algorithms and instructions.
- AI models.
- Training and learning from data.
- Predictions and outputs.

**Planned activities:**
- Match key terms with their definitions.
- Explore a simple example of how data can help an AI model identify patterns.
- Arrange the stages of a basic AI process in the correct order.

**Expected learning outcome:** Learners can explain basic AI terminology and describe a simplified AI workflow.

**Status:** Outline only.

### Section 3: Different Approaches to AI

**File:** `02-ai-approaches.md`

**Purpose:** Introduce different approaches researchers have used to build AI systems.

**Topics and subtopics:**
- **Symbolic AI:** Uses explicit rules, symbols, and logical representations.
- **Connectionist AI:** Uses networks of connected computational units, such as artificial neural networks.
- **Behavior-based AI:** Emphasises producing intelligent behaviour through interactions with an environment.

**Planned activities:**
- Compare how the approaches might address a simple problem.
- Match descriptions of AI systems to the relevant approach.
- Discuss the strengths and limitations of each approach.

**Expected learning outcome:** Learners can describe the three approaches at a basic level and recognise that AI can be developed in different ways.

**Status:** Outline only.

### Section 4: Generative AI

**File:** `04-generative-ai.md`

**Purpose:** Explain how generative AI creates content and how users interact with these systems.

**Topics and subtopics:**
- What is generative AI?
- Text and image generation.
- Introduction to large language models (LLMs).
- What is a prompt?
- How prompts influence AI-generated responses.
- Common uses and limitations of generative AI.

**Planned activities:**
- Give an AI tool a simple prompt.
- Change the prompt and compare the responses.
- Identify strengths and weaknesses in a generated answer.

**Expected learning outcome:** Learners can explain generative AI, describe the purpose of prompts, and evaluate basic AI-generated content.

**Status:** Outline only.

- AI in education.

### Section 6: Limitations and Risks of AI

**File:** `06-ai-limitations-and-risks.md`

**Topics and subtopics:**

**Planned activities:**
