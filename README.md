
# Botol — AI Overview

A team project for *Foundations of CS & AI* (2026).

Botol is developing beginner-friendly learning materials for the **AI Overview** module. The project consists of three learning resources:

- **Study Guide and Test Bank**
- **PowerPoint Slides**
- **Video Tutorials**

All three resources are developed using one shared body of Markdown learning content. This approach helps maintain consistent explanations, examples, activities, and references across the learning materials.

---

## 1. Module Overview

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is increasingly used in education, healthcare, business, science, and everyday life. However, beginners may not fully understand how AI works, what different AI technologies do, or why AI-generated information should be evaluated carefully.

The **AI Overview** module introduces learners to the fundamental concepts of AI, its major approaches, generative AI, practical applications, limitations, and responsible use.

The module follows a progressive learning sequence:

1. Introduction to Artificial Intelligence
2. Basic AI Concepts
3. Different Approaches to AI
4. Generative AI
5. Applications of AI
6. Limitations and Risks of AI
7. Responsible Use of AI
8. Practical Application and Review

Through explanations, worked examples, diagrams, and learning activities, learners will develop an understanding of what AI can do, how it is used, what its limitations are, and how to evaluate and use it appropriately.

## 2. Intended Learners

- **Target audience:** First-year university students with little or no technical background in computer science or artificial intelligence.
- **Prerequisite knowledge:** Basic computer and internet skills.
- **Programming knowledge:** Not required.
- **Mathematical background:** No advanced mathematics required.

Technical terminology is introduced through simple explanations and practical examples to help beginners understand unfamiliar concepts.

## 3. Learning Outcomes

By the end of this module, learners should be able to:

1. **Explain** the meaning of Artificial Intelligence and identify examples of AI in everyday life.
2. **Describe** basic AI concepts, including data, algorithms, models, training, and predictions.
3. **Distinguish** between symbolic, connectionist, and behavior-based approaches to AI at an introductory level.
4. **Explain** the basic purpose of generative AI, large language models (LLMs), and prompts.
5. **Identify** how AI is applied in different industries and everyday situations.
6. **Recognise** common AI limitations and risks, including inaccurate information, hallucinations, bias, privacy concerns, and reliability issues.
7. **Demonstrate** responsible AI use by checking information, protecting personal data, and following academic integrity rules.
8. **Apply** their knowledge to simple scenarios by identifying appropriate AI uses, evaluating AI-generated answers, and explaining their decisions.

These learning outcomes guide the development of the eight content sections and the three learning resources.

## 7. Content Development and Quality Assurance

The team follows a shared development and review process to improve the accuracy, clarity, and usability of the learning materials.

Our process includes:

1. **Content development:** Write explanations, examples, activities, and answers in Markdown.
2. **Peer review:** Have another team member review each assigned section.
3. **Accuracy checking:** Verify important claims, examples, terminology, and references.
4. **Learner testing:** Ask a teammate to follow selected explanations and activities from a beginner's perspective.
5. **Revision:** Address relevant feedback and record corrections.
6. **Resource development:** Use the reviewed Markdown content as the foundation for the three learning resources.

Content progress and outstanding review tasks are documented in:

- [Content Status](project/content-status.md)
- [Review Notes](review/review-notes.md)
- [Corrections Log](review/corrections-log.md)
- [Project Task List](project/task-list.md)

### Content Status Definitions

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Outline Only | The section structure has been planned, but substantive learning content is not yet complete. |
| Drafted | The initial learning content has been written. |
| Needs Revision | Further corrections or improvements are required. |
| Checked | The content has been reviewed for accuracy, clarity, completeness, and consistency. |

A section should only be marked as checked after its outstanding review issues have been addressed.

## 8. GitHub Collaboration

GitHub is used to manage the team's collaborative development process and maintain a traceable revision history.

The team follows these practices:

- Use branches and pull requests to organise significant changes.
- Avoid committing directly to the main branch after the initial setup.
- Ensure individual contributions are identifiable through GitHub accounts.
- Request another team member to review changes before merging.
- Use meaningful commit messages that describe the changes.
- Update project documentation as work progresses.

The repository's commit history, pull requests, and review records provide evidence of the team's development process.
