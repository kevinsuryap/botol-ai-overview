# Review 2:

- **Content creator:**  Franklin Halim
- **Reviewer:**  Ethan Salim
- **Date:**  23/10/2026

## What was checked

- Accuracy of explanations of rule-based systems, machine
  learning, deep learning, and generative AI.
- Correctness of comparisons between the different approaches.
- Clarity of examples and the school support system scenario.
- Whether learners can understand when different approaches
  may be appropriate.
- Whether activities and answer keys match the lesson.
- Accuracy and relevance of references.
- Whether diagrams, file references, and supporting assets work.

## Findings

- Overall structure and clarity: The lesson is well-organized and beginner-friendly. Definitions, examples, comparisons, and activities follow a logical sequence that supports the stated learning outcomes.
- Technical accuracy: The explanations of machine learning, deep learning, and generative AI are generally accurate. The lesson correctly identifies deep learning as a specialised type of machine learning and explains that generative AI can overlap with other approaches.
- Rule-based examples: The password-reset guide and delivery-fee calculator clearly demonstrate predefined rules. However, these examples are not necessarily AI applications. Consider using a rule-based troubleshooting or expert system that applies several logical conditions to recommend a solution.
- Examples and answer key: The examples generally match the approaches being taught. For the animal-image recognition activity, clarify that the system uses a deep learning model, since image recognition can also use other machine learning techniques.
- Practical scenario and responsible use: The school support scenario demonstrates how different approaches can complement one another. The discussion of privacy, bias, reliability, and human oversight is relevant and appropriate.
- References: The IBM machine learning and deep learning resources are relevant and accessible. However, the listed NIST Artificial Intelligence link currently redirects to a page about super intelligence, making it less suitable as a general introductory AI reference. The references should be updated and the citation format checked before publication.

## Corrections or confirmations

- Confirmed the explanation that deep learning is a subset of machine learning and that generative AI can use deep learning.
- Recommended clarifying that predefined-rule automation is not automatically an AI application.
- Recommended refining the animal-image recognition example to specify the use of a deep learning model.
- Recommended replacing the NIST link with a more directly relevant resource, such as the [NIST Generative AI glossary](https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/genai).
- Recommended adding appropriate in-text citations to technical explanations and ensuring that all references follow the citation format required by the course.
- Confirmed that the learner activity and answer key are generally consistent with the lesson content.

## Review 3: Basic AI Concepts

- **Content creator:** Kevin Surya
- **Reviewer:** Bienvenindo
- **Date:** 23/10/2026

### What Was Checked

- Accuracy of explanations of data, algorithms, models, training, and predictions.
- Correctness of the AI workflow diagram.
- Accuracy and clarity of the spam detector worked example.
- Whether technical terms are explained before they are used.
- Whether the activity can be completed using the lesson.
- Whether the answer key is correct.
- Accuracy and relevance of references.
- Whether diagrams, file references, and supporting assets work.

### Findings

The section is well-organised and generally suitable for beginners. The definitions are clear, and the spam detector example effectively demonstrates how data, algorithms, models, training, and predictions work together. The workflow diagram presents a logical sequence, and the matching activity and answer key are consistent. The cat-and-dog application activity also includes expected answers.

Minor improvements are recommended. The difference between an algorithm and a model could be explained using a simpler analogy. The term "generalisation" in the answer key could be defined for beginners. References are provided, but citations could be added beside relevant explanations to show which sources support the claims. The exported document and links should also be tested.

### Corrections or Confirmations

The matching activity answer key was checked and found to be consistent with the definitions provided. The spam detector example appropriately explains that predictions are not guaranteed to be correct. The workflow is identified as a simplified machine learning process, which helps avoid implying that every AI system follows the same steps.

Recommended improvements are to clarify the algorithm-versus-model distinction, explain "generalisation," and add relevant citations. The diagram, references, and exported document should be checked in the final submitted copy.
